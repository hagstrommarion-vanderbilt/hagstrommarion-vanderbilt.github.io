My research connects mechanical design and control with the practical needs of people who use lower-limb prostheses.

Affordable microprocessor knee (AMPK)

Prosthetic design · Hydraulics · Accessibility

Microprocessor-controlled knees can offer valuable functionality, but cost and physical fit can limit access. My doctoral work focuses on developing an affordable, compact knee that preserves essential microprocessor-controlled behavior.

The project includes hydraulic-system design, fabrication, controller development, and benchtop validation, with human-subject evaluation as part of the development pathway. Affordability, build height, and consistent behavior guide the engineering decisions.

Backward-walking control

Semi-powered prostheses · Swing assistance · Gait biomechanics

Backward stepping is part of everyday movement, yet it creates distinct challenges for prosthetic-knee control. I investigate how a swing-assist knee can support this activity through activity-specific control.

This work evaluates knee motion, foot clearance, and compensatory hip movement to understand the value of assistance during backward walking.

Movement across activities

Forward walking · Backward walking · Stair ascent

I am also investigating gait performance across activities with a semi-powered prosthetic knee. This ongoing work examines movement symmetry and the relationship between control behavior and user biomechanics.

Earlier research: brain networks

As an undergraduate researcher in the Nino Research Group at the University of Florida, I used MATLAB and graph theory to model brain connectivity and study potential biomarkers for Alzheimer’s disease.

Assistive design beyond the knee

My broader project work includes modular accessible gaming controllers, a limb attachment with interchangeable implements, and an EMG-controlled robotic hand. These projects extend my interest in accessible technology to a wider range of everyday activities.

Headbands for pediatric hearing assessment

Fabrication · Pediatric audiology · Ongoing collaboration

I am fabricating headbands for a capstone project in Vanderbilt’s Doctor of Audiology (AuD) program. The project addresses a practical challenge during pediatric bone-conduction hearing assessments: headbands can slip or fall off, making it difficult to keep the bone conductor in place.

My contribution focuses on fabricating headbands intended to help maintain the bone conductor’s position while children undergo assessment.

Wearable design at the Venice Biennale

Collaborative garment design · Wond’ry · 2026

Through Vanderbilt’s Wearable Product Design course, led by Alexandra Sargent Capps, I helped create a collaborative robe for artist María Magdalena Campos-Pons to wear at the 2026 Venice Biennale. I traveled to Venice to assist with final fittings and served as a stage assistant.

The experience brought together engineering, textile craft, and performance—and strengthened my interest in the relationship between form, function, and the experience of wearing a device.
